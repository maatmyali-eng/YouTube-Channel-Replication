# Principles and the Copy Line

> **Purpose:** Set honest expectations about monetization and draw the line between copying a channel's style and copying its content.  
> **Use when:** Before building anything, and whenever you are unsure whether a choice crosses the line.  
> **Time needed:** 25 minutes to read, 5 minutes for the self-check prompt each time you doubt a draft.  
> **Needs:** Your filled setup card from [01-setup-card.md](01-setup-card.md).  
> **Cost:** $0

## Inputs

- [CHANNEL_NAME] and [NICHE] from your setup card.
- Later, any draft script, thumbnail description, or title you want to check against the line.

## Steps

1. Read "The reality check" and write down on your card or notes which parts of monetization you control and which you do not.
2. Read "The Inspired-By-Not-Copied line" in full. It is short and every later folder depends on it.
3. Study the 20-row table. For each row, find the nearest decision you will make for your channel and note which column it falls in.
4. Copy the eight golden rules into the top of your notes file so they are in front of you while you work.
5. When a draft feels close to the reference, run P-START-02 below on that draft.

## The reality check

No system can deliver "full-proof" monetization. Monetization is a decision made by YouTube against its own program rules, and those rules change. What a system can do is remove the reasons channels are refused or demonetized and raise the odds that a channel is accepted and stays accepted. That is the only meaning of "monetization quality" in this repo.

What you do not control:

- Whether YouTube accepts your application, and how long review takes.
- How many people the recommendation system shows a video to.
- Advertiser demand in your niche, which changes the revenue per thousand views.
- Policy updates after the date of this repo.

What you do control, and what this repo teaches:

- Meeting the published eligibility thresholds. Here is the current picture as of the research snapshot. Tier 1 (fan funding and shopping features) needs 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days. Tier 2 (ads and the Premium revenue share) needs 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days (verified 2026-10-20). From 1 February 2027, new applicants to Tier 2 need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views, and Shorts revenue for members then requires 10 million Shorts views per rolling 90 days. Applying before that date uses the lower thresholds (verified 2026-10-20). Shorts views and long-form watch hours never combine, and Shorts watch time does not count toward the watch-hour path (verified 2026-10-20). VERIFY BEFORE RELYING on the YouTube Help page "YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility" before you plan your dates.
- Avoiding the "inauthentic content" categories. YouTube's clarification of 16 July 2026 describes three non-monetizable categories: generic, repetitive, or template-based content with little variation between videos; off-putting or distressing content; and AI personas discussing sensitive topics such as health and finance. It targets mass production, not AI use itself. AI voiceover can qualify when the video carries original commentary, education, or entertainment value. The separate reused-content policy did not change (verified 2026-10-20). This is why every folder pushes you to add something that is yours: footage you film, a method you test, a viewpoint, a teaching structure.
- Disclosure and honesty in titles, thumbnails, and sponsorships. See [03-compliance-master-checklist.md](03-compliance-master-checklist.md).
- Consistent quality over the first weeks, measured by viewer behavior, not by upload count.

This repo makes no income claim. Sections in 08-monetization show arithmetic and scenarios, and they are labeled as scenarios, not forecasts. A new channel can reach zero revenue for months. Plan your finances so that outcome is survivable.

## The Inspired-By-Not-Copied line

When this repo says "copy a channel," it means copy the style system. A style system is the set of repeatable choices that make a channel feel like itself: tone of voice, how a script is structured, how a hook is built in the first seconds, how pacing and cuts are timed, where open loops are placed and paid off, how sound is layered under speech, which colors and type are used, how a thumbnail is composed, how titles are phrased, how a series is organized, and how often videos come out. You study these by watching public videos and writing down the pattern, then you apply the pattern to your own subject matter.

What you never copy is the expression: the creator's actual sentences, jokes, narration, footage, music, thumbnail images, logos, mascots, characters, or likeness. Expression is what copyright and YouTube's reused-content rules protect. Style is the method used to produce it, and methods are free for anyone to use.

A practical test: if you removed the reference channel from the world, would your video still work as a coherent original piece? If your script only makes sense as a rewrite of theirs, with their examples in their order, you have copied expression even if every word is different. The fix is to change the content, not the synonyms: pick different examples, test something yourself, make a different argument, film your own footage, and keep only the structural pattern.

A second test is about distance. Close paraphrase is still copying. Moving from "they open with a surprising number, then a promise, then a proof" to "I open with a surprising number from my own kitchen test, then a promise, then my proof" is fine. Moving from their sentence to a reworded version of their sentence is not.

A third consideration is monetization itself. YouTube demonetizes copied and mass-produced content, not shared styles (verified 2026-10-20). Many successful channels in a niche share a style. What they do not share is the same footage, the same words, or an interchangeable template stamped out dozens of times with only the topic swapped. The line in this repo is therefore both the legal line and the commercial line.

There is no neutral third zone where "a little" of the protected expression is acceptable. Short quotations for commentary or criticism can be legal in some places under fair use or similar rules, but whether a use qualifies depends on facts and country, and it is not something this repo will advise you to rely on. If you want to discuss a specific work, quote it minimally, say clearly what you are criticizing, add your own analysis, and treat it as a legal-risk decision you make with eyes open. For everything else in this repo, the rule is: pattern yes, expression no.

## The 20-row do/don't table

Left column: style elements you may study and use. Right column: protected expression you must not use.

| # | Area | Allowed: style element | Not allowed: protected expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Opening with a bold, specific claim and proving it within 10 seconds | Reading or lightly rewording the reference's actual hook line |
| 2 | Script structure | Using "problem, promise, proof, steps, payoff" as your outline | Following their script paragraph by paragraph with reworded sentences |
| 3 | Tone | Dry, deadpan humor delivered at a similar rhythm | Reusing their catchphrases, running jokes, or sign-offs |
| 4 | Voice | A calm, mid-pace narration with a similar energy level | Cloning their voice, or a sound-alike made to be mistaken for them |
| 5 | Pacing | A new visual or cut every 3 to 5 seconds, if that is their measured rhythm | Using their footage or screen captures to match the timing |
| 6 | Open loops | Teasing a result at 0:30 and paying it off near the end | Teasing the same specific mystery or twist they used |
| 7 | Color palette | A warm yellow, charcoal, and cream palette | Their logo, channel art, mascot, or brand marks |
| 8 | Typography | A bold condensed sans-serif for on-screen words | Copying a custom or licensed lettering set you have no license for |
| 9 | Thumbnail composition | Subject on the right, three-word label on the left, high contrast | Tracing, recoloring, or editing their actual thumbnail image |
| 10 | Title pattern | A "number + constraint + outcome" structure | Their exact title, or their title with one word swapped |
| 11 | Series structure | A recurring weekly series with numbered episodes and a fixed segment order | Their series name, episode list, or trademarked format name |
| 12 | Music | Choosing a track of similar mood and tempo from a licensed or royalty-free source | Using their track, ripping audio from their video, or a near copy of their melody |
| 13 | Sound effects | A light "pop" sound when text appears | Extracting their sound effects from their uploads |
| 14 | Topic and ideas | Covering the same general subject their audience cares about | Re-making their video with the same examples and the same order |
| 15 | Intro structure | A 5-second cold open before a short title card | Their intro animation, sting, or animated logo |
| 16 | Facts and data | Using public facts and re-checking them from primary sources | Copying their research compilation, charts, or graphics |
| 17 | Persona | A friendly, practical home-cook host archetype | Their named character, mascot, costume, or likeness |
| 18 | Captions | Word-by-word highlighted captions in a similar layout | Transcribing their audio and recaptioning it as your own video |
| 19 | Format | A ranking or tier-list format with your own tests and opinions | Re-uploading their clips with light narration over the top |
| 20 | Camera setup | An overhead hands-only kitchen shot, if that fits your space | Using their frames or b-roll, or screenshots from their videos |

The Shorts-specific version of rows 19 and 20 is worth stating plainly: cutting vertical clips out of a creator's long video and posting them is reuse, not style. 03-shorts covers how to build Shorts from your own material.

## The owner's golden rules

1. Study the pattern, never lift the words.
2. Every video contains something only I made: footage, a test, a method, or a viewpoint.
3. If it could be swapped into a template with only the topic changed, it is not ready.
4. Disclose realistic synthetic content, and never fake a person, a voice, or a face.
5. Titles and thumbnails promise only what the video delivers.
6. Use only music, footage, and fonts whose license I have read and saved.
7. A human reviews every video before it goes live.
8. I make no income promises to myself or anyone else; I track the numbers weekly instead.

## Prompts

### P-START-02 — Copy-Line Self-Check

**Purpose:** Test a draft script, title, or thumbnail description against the Inspired-By-Not-Copied line and flag where it sits too close to the reference.

**Inputs:** [CHANNEL_NAME], [NICHE], [STYLE_BIBLE] (or write "not built yet"), and the draft to check, called [DRAFT_TEXT]. [DRAFT_TEXT] is the script, title, or thumbnail description you want reviewed. Also provide [REFERENCE_EXCERPT], a short passage of 150 words or fewer from the reference video's on-page transcript that you suspect your draft resembles. Write "none" if you have no specific passage.

```text
You are an originality reviewer for a YouTube channel in the niche [NICHE]. I model the style of the channel [CHANNEL_NAME]. My Style Bible, which lists the style patterns I intend to use, is:

[STYLE_BIBLE]

Below is my draft, followed by a short reference excerpt for comparison.

DRAFT:
[DRAFT_TEXT]

REFERENCE EXCERPT (analysis only):
[REFERENCE_EXCERPT]

Apply the copy line: style patterns (tone, structure, hook mechanics, pacing, open loops, visuals, title pattern, cadence) are allowed; protected expression (actual wording, jokes, catchphrases, examples in the same order, footage, music, logos, characters, likeness) is not.

Produce:
1. VERDICT: one of Clear, Too Close, or Unclear, plus one sentence of reason.
2. EVIDENCE TABLE: columns Draft passage (quote up to 20 words), What it resembles (one of: same wording, close paraphrase, same example, same sequence, same joke or catchphrase, acceptable style pattern), Action (Keep, Rewrite with new content, Delete).
3. STRUCTURE CHECK: list which style patterns from my Style Bible the draft uses correctly, and name any pattern that is applied by copying the reference's specific content instead.
4. ORIGINAL VALUE CHECK: name the single element in the draft that only I could have made (my footage, my test, my opinion, my data). If there is none, say "No original element found" and list three concrete original elements I could add that cost $0.
5. REWRITE BRIEF: for each Rewrite or Delete row, a one-sentence instruction that changes the content, not just the wording.

Do not rewrite the draft for me. Do not reproduce more than 20 words of the reference excerpt in your answer.
```

**Expected output:** A verdict, an evidence table with actions, a structure check, one named original element or three suggested ones, and a rewrite brief.

**If the output is weak:** Give it a longer draft excerpt or a second reference passage, and add the line "Be strict: treat close paraphrase as Too Close." If the verdict is Unclear, also run the scoring rubric in [../05-originality-layer/02-scoring-rubric.md](../05-originality-layer/02-scoring-rubric.md).

## Done when

- You can state in one sentence what "monetization quality" means in this repo and what it does not promise.
- You can explain the difference between style and expression without looking at the table.
- You have copied the eight golden rules to your notes file.
- You have run P-START-02 on at least one real draft once you have one, and every Too Close row has an action.
