# Style Bible Worked Example: "Ledger Kitchen" (Invented)

> **Purpose:** Show a fully filled Style Bible and how it changes a script prompt's output.  
> **Use when:** You want to see what "done" looks like before filling your own bible.  
> **Time needed:** 20 minutes to read.  
> **Needs:** 03-style-bible-template.md open beside it.  
> **Cost:** $0  

**This is an invented example.** "Ledger Kitchen" is not a real channel. Its source channel, its numbers, and its sample outputs are made up for demonstration in the neutral niche "home cooking on a budget". Do not copy its rules into your channel; derive yours from your own analysis.

## Inputs

- The template in [03-style-bible-template.md](03-style-bible-template.md).
- For the before/after: a script prompt (a simplified stand-in for P-LONG-04 in [../02-long-form/02-script-prompt-chain.md](../02-long-form/02-script-prompt-chain.md)).
- Demonstration values: [NICHE] = home cooking on a budget; [AUDIENCE] = adults cooking for one to four people on a tight grocery budget; [LANGUAGE] = English; [FACE_OR_FACELESS] = hands-only on camera, no face; [VOICE_MODE] = own recorded voice; [HARDWARE] = one phone and a low-cost lavalier microphone; [VIDEO_LENGTH] = 8 to 10 minutes.

## Steps

1. Read the filled bible below section by section. Notice that each rule can be checked with a yes or no.
2. Read the before/after comparison to see how the bible changes a prompt's output.
3. Open your own `style-bible.md` and compare: does every field have an equally concrete rule?
4. Run P-CDNA-09 on one of your drafted scripts to test fit against your bible.

### The filled bible (invented)

```markdown
# Channel Style Bible: Ledger Kitchen
Version: v1 (example)
Model channel studied: an invented budget-cooking channel (analysis only)
Niche: home cooking on a budget   Audience: adults cooking for 1 to 4 people on a tight grocery budget   Language: English
Presentation: hands-only, no face   Voice: own recorded voice
Evidence confidence: medium (invented example: 10 transcripts, watch notes on 5 videos)

## 1. Positioning and promise
Source pattern: Shows every price on screen and compares to takeout. (INFERRED)
Our rule: "Real meals, with the receipt on screen." Each video shows the full grocery receipt, the cost per serving, and one swap that saves more.

## 2. Audience
Source pattern: Viewers ask for substitutions and leftovers plans. (OBSERVED in comment themes)
Our rule: Assume a viewer owns a pan, a pot, and a knife but no specialty gear. Explain any technique in one sentence the first time.

## 3. Voice and diction
Source pattern: Calm, dry, second person. (INFERRED)
Our rule: First person plural for cooking ("we"), second person for choices ("you pick"). Contractions always. Reading level around grade 7. Ban: "amazing", "game-changer", "hack", "secret".

## 4. Sentence rhythm
Source pattern: Short sentences with an occasional longer explanatory one. (INFERRED)
Our rule: Most sentences 6 to 14 words. One sentence of 20+ words per minute at most, used only for a reason ("because the starch...").

## 5. Pacing
Source pattern: UNKNOWN (no audio data).
Our rule: PROPOSED, to test: 150 spoken words per minute, a half-second pause after every cost reveal.

## 6. Hook types
Source pattern: Result-first and receipt-first. (OBSERVED in 7 of 10 sampled openings)
Our rule: Primary: receipt-first ("The whole dinner cost this much"). Backup: problem-first. First 5 seconds: the finished dish plus the total cost. First 30 seconds: promise exactly what we cook, for how many servings, and what the viewer can reuse.

## 7. Structure beats
Source pattern: Hook, cost reveal, shopping list, cook, taste, cost recap. (OBSERVED)
Our rule: Hook 0 to 5%. Promise and receipt 5 to 15%. Cook in 3 segments 15 to 75%. One mid-video re-hook (a price surprise) at about 50%. Taste test and per-serving math 75 to 92%. Leftover plan and next video 92 to 100%.

## 8. Open-loop habits
Source pattern: Teases the cheapest swap and reveals it late. (INFERRED)
Our rule: Two loops per video. Loop 1 ("the swap that saves the most") opens in the hook and closes at 70%. Loop 2 opens at the re-hook and closes in the recap. No unpaid loops.

## 9. Humor and tone rules
Source pattern: Understated, dry. (INFERRED)
Our rule: One light joke per 2 minutes at most, about the situation and never about people. When a dish fails, say what went wrong and what we change.

## 10. Visual style
Source pattern: Overhead hands-only shot plus close-ups. (OBSERVED in notes)
Our rule: Main shot overhead on a fixed phone mount. Close-up inserts of at least 30% of cooking time. Receipt graphic at the start, price tags on screen when an ingredient first appears.

## 11. Color and typography
Source pattern: UNKNOWN.
Our rule: Palette: cream #F4E9D8, deep green #1F3A2E, tomato #E4572E. Fonts: Anton for numbers, Fraunces for headings, both from Google Fonts (open licenses; VERIFY BEFORE RELYING on each font's page). Text at most 7 words on screen at once.

## 12. Editing rhythm
Source pattern: A cut or insert every few seconds. (OBSERVED: 12 cuts in a 60-second sample)
Our rule: No more than 8 seconds without a visual change or an on-screen price. Only hard cuts and quick fades. Captions always on, white text with a dark outline.

## 13. Audio and music design
Source pattern: Light background music, kitchen sounds kept. (OBSERVED in notes)
Our rule: Voice recorded with the lavalier. Kitchen sounds kept low. One royalty-free track from the YouTube Audio Library per video, license checked per track (VERIFY BEFORE RELYING). Music 18 to 20 dB below the voice. No sound effects except a soft tick on price reveals.

## 14. CTA style
Source pattern: One subscribe line near the end. (OBSERVED)
Our rule: One CTA only, at the recap: "If you want next week's receipt, subscribe." No begging, no fake urgency.

## 15. Series structure
Source pattern: Weekly theme series. (OBSERVED)
Our rule: Series "The $X Week" (one price ceiling, one theme). Each episode: same beat map, different constraint.

## 16. Title formulas
Source pattern: "[Meal] for $[N]" and "[N] Days of [Constraint]". (OBSERVED)
Our rule: 1) "[Meal] for [N] People, Receipt Included" 2) "I Cooked [N] Dinners From One $[N] Shop" 3) "The Cheapest Way to Make [Dish], Tested" 4) "[Dish]: [Cheap Version] vs [Takeout Version]". Maximum 60 characters. Every number in a title must appear on the receipt in the video.

## 17. Thumbnail formulas
Source pattern: One food close-up and a price badge. (INFERRED from notes)
Our rule: Three elements at most: the finished dish, a price badge, at most 3 words. Palette from section 11. The dish and price must match the video exactly.

## 18. Description, chapters, and metadata habits
Source pattern: Chapters per cooking segment. (OBSERVED)
Our rule: Chapters named by step ("Shop", "Cook the base", "Cost recap"). First two description lines state the dish, servings, and total cost.

## 19. Taboo list
- Reusing another creator's footage, audio, thumbnails, or script wording
- Mimicking a real person's voice or likeness
- Deceptive titles, thumbnails, or metadata
- Videos that differ only by topic and voiceover
- The model channel's signature price-badge shape and its recurring tagline
- Health or medical claims (weight loss, curing, "healthy" claims) and diet advice
- Prices we have not shown on a receipt

## 20. What we will do differently
1. Show the full receipt and the arithmetic, not just the total.
2. Cook each recipe twice and report which version we would repeat.
3. Add a leftovers plan with the cost of day two.
4. Add a substitutions table for the 3 priciest ingredients.
5. Publish the price list with the shop date so viewers can compare to their own area.
6. Open each video with a tested failure or mistake when there was one.

## 21. Compliance guardrails
- Disclosure: our footage is real and our voice is ours, so no altered or synthetic content setting applies; if we ever add synthetic visuals that look real, we set "Altered or synthetic content" (VERIFY BEFORE RELYING at YouTube Help).
- Sensitive topics: no health claims, no nutrition advice beyond ingredient facts.
- Sponsorships and affiliate links: disclose in the first description line and in the video.

## 22. Unknowns to verify
- Pacing: count words per minute in 3 sampled videos.
- Color and typography of the model channel: not needed, our palette is our own.
- Whether the model channel's first-30-second promise wording varies: watch 3 more openings.
```

### Before and after: how the bible feeds a script prompt

This is illustrative. The outputs below are written by hand to show what changes, not real model outputs.

**Prompt without the bible:**

```
Write the opening 30 seconds of a YouTube script about cheap rice and beans meals for a video of about 9 minutes.
```

**Typical result (illustrative):**

```
Are you tired of spending too much on groceries? In today's video, we're going to look at some amazing rice and bean recipes that will save you tons of money. Make sure to like and subscribe, and let's get started!
```

Problems: no concrete promise, no proof, a CTA in the first seconds, banned words ("amazing"), and nothing that distinguishes the channel.

**Prompt with the bible:**

```
You are the scriptwriter for [CHANNEL_NAME]. Follow the STYLE BIBLE exactly. Do not copy any other creator's wording.

STYLE BIBLE:
[STYLE_BIBLE]

Task: Write the opening 30 seconds of a script for the video topic [VIDEO_TOPIC] with length [VIDEO_LENGTH]. Use the primary hook type from section 6, open Loop 1 from section 8, respect sections 3 to 5 for diction and rhythm, and include on-screen text instructions per section 11. After the script, list which bible rules you applied.
```

**Typical result (illustrative):**

```
[Overhead shot: three plates of rice and beans. On screen: "$11.40 total, 4 dinners"]
This is four dinners for eleven dollars forty. Here's the receipt.
[Receipt graphic, price tags tick in.]
We're cooking one pot of beans three ways, and one of those ways costs half as much per plate as the others. You'll see which at the seven-minute mark.
```

What changed: the first five seconds show the dish and the total (section 6), Loop 1 opens with a payoff time (section 8), sentences run 6 to 14 words (section 4), contractions and "we" and "you" appear (section 3), and there is no CTA (section 14). The numbers in this sample are invented for the demonstration.

## Prompts

### P-CDNA-09 — Bible Fit Test

Purpose: Check whether a draft script or title obeys your Style Bible and report violations.
Inputs: [STYLE_BIBLE], [DRAFT] (a script, hook, title set, or thumbnail description), [DRAFT_TYPE] (script, hook, titles, or thumbnail).

```
You are a strict editor. Check the draft against the Style Bible. Do not rewrite the whole draft.

STYLE BIBLE:
[STYLE_BIBLE]

DRAFT TYPE: [DRAFT_TYPE]
DRAFT:
[DRAFT]

Output:
1. A table of every Our rule that applies to this draft type, with PASS, FAIL, or NOT APPLICABLE and one sentence of evidence quoting at most 8 words from the draft.
2. For each FAIL, the minimal change that fixes it, written out.
3. A taboo check: confirm none of the section 19 items appears, or list each one that does.
4. A similarity warning: flag any phrase that reads like a catchphrase or distinctive wording from another creator, and propose an original alternative.
5. A final verdict: READY, FIX THEN READY, or REWRITE.
```

Expected output: A rule-by-rule table, minimal fixes, a taboo check, a similarity check, and a verdict.
If the output is weak: Paste only sections 3 to 8 and 14 of the bible for a script, or sections 16 and 17 for titles and thumbnails, and rerun.
